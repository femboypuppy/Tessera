NASA's program to return people to the Moon, this time to stay, with international and commercial partners. In Greek mythology, Artemis is Apollo's twin sister (see [[Apollo program]]).

## The missions

- **Artemis I** (November 16 – December 11, 2022): an uncrewed test. The new SLS rocket launched an Orion spacecraft around the Moon and back.
- **Artemis II**: four astronauts, Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, Christina Koch and Jeremy Hansen of the Canadian Space Agency, fly around the Moon, the first crewed lunar flight since 1972.
- **Artemis III**: aims to land astronauts near the lunar south pole, where craters in permanent shadow hold water ice.

## What's different from Apollo

- **Where:** Apollo landed near the equator; Artemis targets the south pole, for the ice. Water means drinking water, air and rocket fuel made on the Moon.
- **Who:** the crews include the first woman and the first person of color to fly to the Moon.
- **How:** the landers are built by commercial companies, and plans have included a small station in lunar orbit.

> [!note] Dates move
> Space programs slip. Check NASA's Artemis pages for the current schedule before quoting a date on the exhibit.

The SLS is the first NASA rocket since the [[Saturn V]] built to send crews to the Moon, and it has even more thrust at liftoff. The landers are designed around [[Reusable rockets]].
