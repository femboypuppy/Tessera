# Sputnik 1

The first artificial satellite. The Soviet Union launched it on **October 4, 1957**, from the
Baikonur Cosmodrome on an R-7 rocket designed by [[Sergei Korolev]]'s bureau.

| Fact            | Value                                                   |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Shape           | A polished metal sphere, 58 cm across, with four antennas |
| Mass            | 83.6 kg                                                 |
| Orbit           | About 96 minutes per orbit                              |
| Transmitted     | Radio beeps for 21 days, until its batteries ran out    |
| Re-entered      | January 4, 1958                                         |

## Why it mattered

Anyone with a shortwave radio could hear Sputnik's beeps, and its rocket stage could be seen
crossing the night sky. In the United States the shock became known as the "Sputnik crisis." It
led to the creation of NASA in 1958 and started the [[Space Race]].

A month later, Sputnik 2 carried the first animal into orbit: [[Laika]].

> [!info] What was inside
> Almost nothing: batteries, two radio transmitters, a fan and temperature sensors. Engineers
> kept it simple on purpose so it could fly before the more ambitious satellite they were
> building was ready.
