The largest telescope ever sent to space. JWST launched on **December 25, 2021**, on an Ariane 5 rocket from Kourou, French Guiana. It is a partnership of NASA, ESA and the Canadian Space Agency.

| Fact       | Value                                                                  |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mirror     | 6.5 m across: 18 hexagonal segments of gold-coated beryllium           |
| Sunshield  | Five layers, about the size of a tennis court                          |
| Location   | Near the Sun–Earth L2 point, about 1.5 million km from Earth           |
| Sees       | Infrared light                                                         |

## Why infrared

Light from the first galaxies has been stretched by the expansion of the universe into infrared wavelengths. Infrared also passes through dust clouds where stars and planets form. To see it, the telescope must be very cold, which is why it hides behind its sunshield, far from Earth's heat.

## Unfolding in space

The telescope was too big to launch open, so it launched folded and unfolded itself over two weeks, with hundreds of mechanisms that each had to work the first time. Engineers called them "single-point failures."

## First images

The first full-color images were released on **July 12, 2022**, including a deep field full of galaxies whose light has traveled for billions of years.

Webb works alongside the [[Hubble Space Telescope]], which sees mostly visible light. Getting to L2 took a month of careful orbital mechanics; [[Hohmann transfer orbit]] explains the basics.
