The Saturn V was the rocket that sent astronauts to the Moon in the [[Apollo program]]. Designed under [[Wernher von Braun]] at NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center, it is still one of the most powerful rockets ever flown.

| Fact          | Value                                                        |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Height        | 110.6 m, about the height of a 36-story building             |
| Stages        | Three, burning one after another                             |
| First stage   | Five F-1 engines, about 34 meganewtons of thrust together    |
| Flights       | 13, from 1967 to 1973                                        |
| Payloads lost | None                                                         |

## Why so big

To reach the Moon, a spacecraft needs to go fast enough to escape most of Earth's gravity. Almost everything that left the launch pad was propellant. The three astronauts came home in a capsule about 3.9 m across; the rest of the 2,900-tonne stack was left behind in stages along the way.

## The last flight

The final Saturn V launched Skylab, America's first space station, in May 1973. Three unused Saturn Vs are on display today, in Houston, Huntsville and at the Kennedy Space Center.

> [!info] A roar you could feel
> Spectators watched launches from about 5 km away. Even there, the sound shook their chests.

Its successor for deep space is the SLS rocket of the [[Artemis program]].
