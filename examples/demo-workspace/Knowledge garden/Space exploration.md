## The race to orbit

The [[Space Race]] began when the Soviet Union launched [[Sputnik 1]] in 1957. A month later
[[Laika]] became the first animal to orbit Earth. In 1961 [[Yuri Gagarin]] became the first
human in space, and in 1963 [[Valentina Tereshkova]] became the first woman. Behind the Soviet
successes stood one secretive engineer: [[Sergei Korolev]].

The United States answered with [[Project Mercury]] and [[Project Gemini]], then the
[[Apollo program]], built around [[Wernher von Braun]]'s [[Saturn V]] and the orbital
calculations of mathematicians like [[Katherine Johnson]].

## To the Moon

- [[Apollo 11]]: [[Neil Armstrong]] and Buzz Aldrin walk on the Moon, July 1969.
- [[Apollo 13]]: "a successful failure" that brought its crew home against the odds.
- [[Artemis program]]: going back, this time to stay.

## Living in orbit

From [[Mir]] to the [[International Space Station]], people have lived in space without a break
since 2000. The [[Space Shuttle]] carried much of the station up, one module at a time. Today,
[[Reusable rockets]] are changing what a launch costs.

## Robots and telescopes

- [[Voyager program]]: two spacecraft still talking to us from interstellar space.
- [[Cassini–Huygens]]: thirteen years at Saturn and a landing on Titan.
- [[New Horizons]]: the first close look at Pluto.
- [[Mars rovers]]: from Sojourner to Perseverance and its helicopter.
- [[Hubble Space Telescope]] and [[James Webb Space Telescope]]: the deepest views of the universe.

## How it works

- [[Hohmann transfer orbit]]: the cheapest way between two orbits.
- [[Gravity assist]]: borrowing speed from a planet.
