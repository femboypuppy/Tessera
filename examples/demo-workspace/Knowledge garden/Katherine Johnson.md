Katherine Johnson (1918–2020) was a NASA mathematician whose calculations of orbital mechanics were critical to the first American crewed spaceflights.

## Work

- Joined NACA, NASA's predecessor, in 1953 as one of the "human computers" at Langley, in the segregated West Area Computing unit.
- Calculated the trajectory for Alan Shepard's 1961 flight, the first American in space ([[Project Mercury]]).
- Before John Glenn's orbital flight in 1962, Glenn asked that the numbers from NASA's new IBM computers be checked by her, by hand. She confirmed them.
- Worked on the calculations that synchronized the [[Apollo 11]] lunar module with the command module in lunar orbit, and on backup procedures that helped bring [[Apollo 13]] home.

## Recognition

She received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2015. The 2016 book and film *Hidden Figures* told her story, with her colleagues Dorothy Vaughan and Mary Jackson. She died on February 24, 2020, at 101.

The [[Hohmann transfer orbit]] and the rest of orbital mechanics was her daily work, done with pencil, paper and mechanical calculators.
