The third planned Moon landing, which became a rescue. Part of the [[Apollo program]].

Crew: Jim Lovell (commander), Jack Swigert and Fred Haise. Launched **April 11, 1970**.

## What went wrong

About 56 hours into the flight, on **April 13, 1970**, an oxygen tank in the service module exploded. It damaged the other oxygen tank and the fuel cells that made the command module's electricity and water.

> [!danger] "Houston, we've had a problem."
> Jack Swigert said it first, then Jim Lovell repeated it. (The film shortened it to "Houston, we have a problem.")

## How they got home

- **A lifeboat.** The crew moved into the lunar module *Aquarius*, built to support two people for about two days, and made it support three for four.
- **The long way round.** Instead of turning back, they used the Moon's gravity to swing around it and head home, a free-return path like a [[Gravity assist]].
- **Duct tape engineering.** Carbon dioxide rose because the command module's square filters didn't fit the lunar module's round sockets. Engineers on the ground designed an adapter from plastic bags, cardboard, a sock and duct tape, and read the instructions up to the crew.
- **Cold and dark.** To save power, most systems were switched off. The cabin fell to a few degrees above freezing.

The crew splashed down safely on **April 17, 1970**. Flight director Gene Kranz and his team became the model for how mission control works under pressure.

> [!success] A successful failure
> No landing, but three people came home from a disaster 300,000 km from Earth.
