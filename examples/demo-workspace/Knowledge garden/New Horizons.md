# New Horizons

NASA's New Horizons gave us the first close look at Pluto. It launched on **January 19, 2006**,
faster than any spacecraft before it, and passed Jupiter only 13 months later, using a
[[Gravity assist]] to speed up.

## Pluto, finally

On **July 14, 2015**, after nine and a half years, New Horizons flew past Pluto at about
12,500 km. The pictures showed a world far more active than anyone expected: mountains of water
ice, a vast heart-shaped plain of nitrogen ice, and a blue haze.

> [!info] Clyde Tombaugh
> The spacecraft carries some of the ashes of Clyde Tombaugh, who discovered Pluto in 1930.

## Arrokoth

On January 1, 2019, it flew past Arrokoth, a small, reddish object shaped like a snowman in the
Kuiper Belt. It is the most distant object ever explored up close, about 6.5 billion km from the
Sun at the time.

## Still flying

New Horizons is heading out of the solar system, following the [[Voyager program]]. Like
[[Cassini–Huygens]], it runs on nuclear power, because sunlight is far too weak out there for
solar panels.
