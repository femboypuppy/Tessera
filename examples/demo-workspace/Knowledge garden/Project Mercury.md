# Project Mercury

Project Mercury (1958–1963) was the first American human spaceflight program. Its goal: put a
person in orbit around Earth and bring them back safely. Six crewed flights flew, each with one
astronaut in a capsule barely larger than a phone booth.

## The Mercury Seven

In April 1959, NASA introduced its first seven astronauts, all military test pilots: Scott
Carpenter, Gordon Cooper, John Glenn, Gus Grissom, Wally Schirra, Alan Shepard and Deke Slayton.

## Key flights

| Date       | Flight       | Astronaut     | What happened                                         |
| ---------- | ------------ | ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| 1961-05-05 | Freedom 7    | Alan Shepard  | First American in space, a 15-minute suborbital hop.  |
| 1962-02-20 | Friendship 7 | John Glenn    | First American in orbit: three orbits in about five hours. |
| 1963-05-15 | Faith 7      | Gordon Cooper | The last and longest flight: 22 orbits, over a day.   |

Before John Glenn's flight, he asked for the orbit computed by NASA's new electronic computers to
be checked by hand by [[Katherine Johnson]]. "If she says they're good," he said, "then I'm ready
to go."

## What came next

Mercury proved people could work in space. [[Project Gemini]] then practiced everything a Moon
mission would need.

Part of the [[Space Race]].
