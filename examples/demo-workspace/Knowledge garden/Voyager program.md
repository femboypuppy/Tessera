Two spacecraft launched in 1977 to fly past the giant planets, and still sending data home from beyond the edge of the solar system.

| Spacecraft | Launched           | Visited                              | Interstellar space since |
| ---------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------ |
| Voyager 2  | August 20, 1977    | Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune     | November 2018            |
| Voyager 1  | September 5, 1977  | Jupiter, Saturn (and its moon Titan) | August 2012              |

## The Grand Tour

In the late 1970s the outer planets lined up in a way that happens about once every 175 years. A spacecraft could use a [[Gravity assist]] at each planet to reach the next. Voyager 2 is still the only spacecraft to have visited Uranus (1986) and Neptune (1989).

## The Golden Record

Each Voyager carries a gold-plated copper record with sounds and images of Earth: greetings in 55 languages, music from Bach to Chuck Berry, and instructions for playing it, in case anyone ever finds it.

## The Pale Blue Dot

On February 14, 1990, at Carl Sagan's urging, Voyager 1 turned around and photographed Earth from about 6 billion km away. Earth is a single pixel in a sunbeam.

> [!quote] Carl Sagan, *Pale Blue Dot* (1994)
> Look again at that dot. That's here. That's home. That's us.

Both spacecraft are running out of power, and instruments are being switched off one by one to keep them talking for as long as possible.

The later [[Cassini–Huygens]] and [[New Horizons]] missions followed Voyager's path outward.
