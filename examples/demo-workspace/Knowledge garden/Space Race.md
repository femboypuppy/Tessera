# Space Race

The Space Race was the competition between the Soviet Union and the United States, from 1957 to
the mid-1970s, to reach space, orbit, and finally the Moon first. It grew out of the Cold War:
rockets that could put a satellite in orbit could also carry a warhead across the world.

## How it unfolded

1. **The Soviet Union leads.** [[Sputnik 1]] (1957), [[Laika]] (1957), [[Yuri Gagarin]] (1961),
   [[Valentina Tereshkova]] (1963) and Alexei Leonov's first spacewalk (1965) were all Soviet
   firsts, directed by [[Sergei Korolev]].
2. **The United States commits to the Moon.** On May 25, 1961, President John F. Kennedy asked
   Congress to land a man on the Moon "before this decade is out." [[Project Mercury]] and
   [[Project Gemini]] built the skills; the [[Apollo program]] did the job.
3. **The Moon.** [[Apollo 11]] landed in July 1969. The Soviet crewed lunar program, built around
   the N1 rocket, failed in four test launches and was cancelled.
4. **Détente.** In July 1975, an Apollo and a Soyuz spacecraft docked in orbit, and their crews
   shook hands through the hatch. Many historians date the end of the race to that handshake.

## Why it matters

The race produced the [[Saturn V]], the first computers small enough to fly, and a generation of
engineers and scientists. It also set the pattern for cooperation that followed: [[Mir]] hosted
American astronauts, and the [[International Space Station]] is run by both former rivals.

## People

- [[Sergei Korolev]] and [[Wernher von Braun]], the two chief rocket designers, never met.
- [[Katherine Johnson]] computed trajectories for Mercury and Apollo.
- [[Neil Armstrong]] and [[Yuri Gagarin]] are the race's two most famous faces.

> We choose to go to the Moon in this decade and do the other things, not because they are easy,
> but because they are hard.
