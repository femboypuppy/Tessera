# Hohmann transfer orbit

The most fuel-efficient way to move between two circular orbits around the same body, using just
two engine burns. Walter Hohmann, a German engineer, described it in 1925, decades before anything
reached orbit.

![[hohmann-transfer.png]]

## The two burns

1. **Speed up** in the inner orbit. The spacecraft rises onto an ellipse that just touches the
   outer orbit on the far side.
2. **Coast** half an ellipse.
3. **Speed up again** at the top, to match the speed of the outer orbit and stay there.

Going inward works the same way, with two braking burns.

## Earth to Mars

A Hohmann-like trip from Earth to Mars takes about **eight and a half months**. It only works when
Mars will be at the far end of the ellipse on arrival, so launch windows open about every **26
months**. That's why missions to Mars, from the [[Mars rovers]] onward, launch in clusters.

> [!tip] Faster costs more
> A faster path needs a lot more fuel. A [[Gravity assist]] can add speed for free, if a planet is
> in the right place.

## In practice

Mission designers like [[Katherine Johnson]] turned ideas like this into exact trajectories.
Spacecraft today fly refined versions of it, but the principle is the same.
