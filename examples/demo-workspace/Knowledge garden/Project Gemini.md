# Project Gemini

Project Gemini (1965–1966) was NASA's bridge between [[Project Mercury]] and the
[[Apollo program]]. Ten crewed flights, each with two astronauts, practiced the skills a trip to
the Moon would need.

## What Gemini proved

- **Long flights.** Gemini 7 stayed in orbit for almost 14 days, longer than a Moon mission.
- **Spacewalks.** Ed White made the first American spacewalk on Gemini 4 in June 1965. (The
  first ever was Alexei Leonov's, from the Soviet Voskhod 2, that March.)
- **Rendezvous.** In December 1965, Gemini 6A and Gemini 7 flew within a few meters of each
  other.
- **Docking.** On Gemini 8, in March 1966, [[Neil Armstrong]] and David Scott made the first
  docking of two spacecraft in orbit.

## Gemini 8: a close call

Shortly after docking with an Agena target vehicle, the joined spacecraft began to roll. After
undocking, the spin got worse, nearly one turn per second, because one of Gemini's own thrusters
was stuck on. Armstrong shut down the main thrusters and used the re-entry thrusters to stop the
spin, which meant the mission had to end early. The calm with which he handled it was one reason he
was later chosen to command [[Apollo 11]].

> [!success] Ready for the Moon
> By the end of 1966, every piece Apollo needed, except the lunar landing itself, had been done
> in Earth orbit.
