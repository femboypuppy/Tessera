# Apollo 11

The first crewed landing on the Moon, part of the [[Apollo program]].

| Role                      | Astronaut                              |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Commander                 | [[Neil Armstrong]]                     |
| Lunar module pilot        | Edwin "Buzz" Aldrin                    |
| Command module pilot      | Michael Collins                        |

Spacecraft: the command module *Columbia* and the lunar module *Eagle*, launched on a
[[Saturn V]] on **July 16, 1969**.

## The landing

On **July 20, 1969**, Armstrong and Aldrin undocked *Eagle* and began their descent while Collins
stayed in lunar orbit in *Columbia*.

- Twice, the guidance computer showed "1202" and "1201" program alarms. It was overloaded, but it
  was designed to drop low-priority work and keep flying. Mission Control called "go."
- The autopilot was steering toward a crater field full of boulders. Armstrong took manual control
  and flew on until he found a safe spot, with less than a minute of fuel left before a mandatory
  abort call.
- At 20:17 UTC: *"Houston, Tranquility Base here. The Eagle has landed."*

## The first steps

At 02:56 UTC on July 21, Armstrong stepped onto the Sea of Tranquility:

> [!quote] Neil Armstrong
> That's one small step for [a] man, one giant leap for mankind.

The two astronauts spent about two and a half hours outside. They planted a flag, set up
experiments (one laser reflector still works and is used to measure the Moon's distance), and
collected 21.5 kg of samples.

## Coming home

*Eagle* lifted off after 21.5 hours on the surface and docked with *Columbia*. The crew splashed down
in the Pacific on **July 24, 1969**, and spent three weeks in quarantine.

Next mission to remember: [[Apollo 13]].
