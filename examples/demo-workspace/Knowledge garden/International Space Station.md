The largest structure humans have built in space, and a home in orbit since **November 2, 2000**: people have lived aboard without a break ever since.

| Fact       | Value                                                              |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Partners   | NASA, Roscosmos, ESA, JAXA and the Canadian Space Agency           |
| First module | Zarya, launched November 20, 1998                                |
| Orbit      | About 400 km up, around Earth every 90 minutes (about 16 times a day) |
| Size       | About the length of a football field, solar arrays included        |

## How it was built

Module by module, like [[Mir]] before it, but bigger. The [[Space Shuttle]] carried most of the pieces, and astronauts connected them on hundreds of hours of spacewalks.

## What happens there

Experiments in biology, physics, medicine and materials that need weightlessness, and research on how long stays in space change the human body. That research is essential for a trip to Mars.

Crews and cargo now arrive on spacecraft launched by [[Reusable rockets]] as well as Russian Soyuz.

> [!tip] Spot it yourself
> The station is often the third-brightest object in the night sky. Space agencies publish when it will pass over your city.

The partners plan to retire the station around 2030 and guide it into a remote part of the ocean.
