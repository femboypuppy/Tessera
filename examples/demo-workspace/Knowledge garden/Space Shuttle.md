NASA's Space Shuttle flew 135 missions between **1981 and 2011**. It was the first partly reusable crewed spacecraft: a winged orbiter that landed like a glider, two solid rocket boosters recovered from the ocean, and an external fuel tank that was thrown away each flight. See [[Reusable rockets]].

## The orbiters

Columbia, Challenger, Discovery, Atlantis and Endeavour flew in space. Enterprise was a test vehicle for approach and landing tests.

## What it did

- Launched the [[Hubble Space Telescope]] in 1990, and flew the five missions that repaired and upgraded it.
- Docked with the Russian station [[Mir]] nine times.
- Carried most of the [[International Space Station]] into orbit, piece by piece.
- Flew the first American woman in space, Sally Ride, in 1983.

## Two tragedies

> [!danger] Challenger and Columbia
> On January 28, 1986, Challenger broke apart 73 seconds after launch when a seal in a solid rocket booster failed in cold weather. On February 1, 2003, Columbia broke apart on re-entry: foam from the external tank had damaged its wing during launch. Fourteen astronauts died.

[[Neil Armstrong]] served on the commission that investigated the Challenger accident.

The first flight, STS-1, launched on April 12, 1981, twenty years to the day after [[Yuri Gagarin]]'s flight. The last, STS-135, landed on July 21, 2011.
