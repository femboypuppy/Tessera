# Sergei Korolev

Sergei Korolev (1907–1966) was the lead rocket engineer and spacecraft designer of the Soviet space
program. His bureau built the R-7 rocket, [[Sputnik 1]], the Vostok spacecraft that carried
[[Yuri Gagarin]] and [[Valentina Tereshkova]], and the early Soyuz.

For almost his whole career his name was a state secret. The world knew him only as "the Chief
Designer." His identity was made public after his death.

## A life in two halves

- In 1938, during Stalin's purges, he was arrested on false charges, tortured, and sent to the
  Kolyma gold mines. He was later moved to a prison design bureau and released in 1944.
- After the war he studied the German V-2, the rocket [[Wernher von Braun]] had designed, and
  went on to build far more powerful ones.

## Legacy

Korolev died on January 14, 1966, after surgery, while the Soviet crewed lunar program was still
being built. Many historians believe his death was one reason the Soviet Union lost the race to the
Moon. The R-7 family he started still flies: every Soyuz launch rides a descendant of it.

Part of the [[Space Race]].
