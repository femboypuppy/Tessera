Wernher von Braun (1912–1977) led the development of the [[Saturn V]], the rocket that took astronauts to the Moon in the [[Apollo program]].

## A difficult history

Before he built rockets for NASA, von Braun built them for Nazi Germany. He was the technical director of the V-2 missile program and a member of the Nazi party and the SS. V-2s were fired at London, Antwerp and other cities, and thousands of concentration camp prisoners died building them in the underground Mittelwerk factory.

At the end of the war he surrendered to the US Army and was brought to the United States with many of his team, in what became known as Operation Paperclip.

> [!warning] Both parts are true
> Any honest account of the Apollo program has to hold both: von Braun's engineering made the Moon landings possible, and the rocket that started his career was built with forced labor.

## In the United States

- His team launched Explorer 1, the first American satellite, in January 1958.
- He became the first director of NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center in 1960.
- The Saturn V flew 13 times, and none of its payloads was lost.

His Soviet counterpart was [[Sergei Korolev]]; the two never met.

Part of the [[Space Race]].
