On **April 12, 1961**, Soviet pilot Yuri Gagarin became the first human in space. His Vostok 1 spacecraft made one orbit of Earth; the flight lasted **108 minutes** from launch to landing.

As the rocket lifted off, he said *"Poyekhali!"*, "Let's go!"

## The flight

- Launched from Baikonur on a rocket designed by [[Sergei Korolev]]'s bureau.
- Vostok was controlled automatically. Gagarin carried a sealed envelope with a code to unlock the manual controls in an emergency.
- As planned, he ejected from the capsule at about 7 km altitude and landed by parachute near the Volga river, where a farmer and her granddaughter were the first people to meet him.

## After

Gagarin became a worldwide celebrity and toured dozens of countries. He never flew in space again: he died in a jet training accident on March 27, 1968, at 34.

April 12 is celebrated as Cosmonautics Day in Russia and as the International Day of Human Space Flight. The first [[Space Shuttle]] launched on the twentieth anniversary of his flight.

Two years later, [[Valentina Tereshkova]] followed him into orbit.
