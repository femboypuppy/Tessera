# Gravity assist

A gravity assist uses a planet's gravity to change a spacecraft's speed and direction without
burning fuel.

## How it works

Relative to the planet, the spacecraft swings around and leaves at the same speed it arrived. But
the planet itself is moving around the Sun. Seen from the Sun, the spacecraft can leave faster (if
it passes behind the planet) or slower (if it passes in front), borrowing a tiny bit of the
planet's orbital energy.

> [!example] A ball and a train
> Throw a ball at the front of an oncoming train. It bounces off faster than you threw it, because
> it picked up the train's speed. A gravity assist does the same thing, without the bounce.

The planet slows down too, but it is so massive that the change is far too small to measure.

## Famous uses

- **Mariner 10** used Venus to reach Mercury in 1974, the first gravity assist.
- The [[Voyager program]] toured all four giant planets this way.
- [[Cassini–Huygens]] swung past Venus twice, Earth and Jupiter to reach Saturn.
- [[New Horizons]] used Jupiter to shorten its trip to Pluto by about three years.
- [[Apollo 13]] used the Moon's gravity on a free-return path to come home.

Gravity assists and the [[Hohmann transfer orbit]] are the two basic tools of interplanetary
travel.
